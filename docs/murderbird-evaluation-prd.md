## 1. Product vision and intended experience

MurderBird: Uncaged is a single-page, science-museum-style encounter with a virtual mechanical creature inside an enclosure. The visitor should feel that a substantial, dangerous machine occupies that space. The visitor can observe, move around it with the camera, approach through an explicit interaction, provoke an era-appropriate response, and understand how its construction works by opening and separating its assemblies.

The original dinosaur/dragon museum-exhibit analogy describes immersive presence and interaction. The raptor analogy describes speed, attention, agitation, and directed aggression in the advanced era. MurderBird is **not a velociraptor**, crow, robin, Tweety Bird, generic steampunk mascot, humanoid robot, or copied franchise character. The intended confidence and power suggest a machine that could plausibly challenge formidable predators within the fiction; this does not require a combat game, enemy dinosaurs, simulated injuries, or quantified combat victory.

The Tin Man and Tik-Tok comparisons distinguish an early operated or mechanical body from later power and cognition. The early/later Iron Man comparison distinguishes constrained machinery from abundant fictional power and coordinated actuation. The B2/Terminator comparisons describe mass, strength, and readable machinery. They must never substitute another character's anatomy, signature reactor, logos, names, or recognizable surface design for MurderBird.

The requested game-quality benchmark is a coherent, polished, readable real-time character with believable motion and persona. Photorealism is not a prerequisite. Fidelity to MurderBird is. A polished but incorrect creature fails.

### Visitor outcomes

1. Recognize the established MurderBird immediately, before reading its name.
2. Recognize three histories of the same inherited body, not three palette swaps or unrelated redesigns.
3. Understand what can move in each era, why it moves, and what it cannot do.
4. Experience an agitated, perceptive, frustrated apex-predator persona in Advanced, with motion that continues between inputs.
5. Explore the exterior and proposed interior without losing the creature's identity or breaking its construction.
6. Return from inspection to a coherent encounter, with accessible controls and optional media.

## 2. Authority, source hierarchy, and conflict resolution

Use the following order for decisions within each source's legitimate scope:

1. The owner's latest explicit direction, including the visual-twin requirement in this conversation.
2. Documented owner selection of a particular image, clip, feature, or experience; retain that selection's exact scope.
3. Current project creative-authority decisions and scoped production reference selections.
4. The preserved story for narrative facts, with explicit exhibit-versus-story distinctions where the owner has changed exhibit behavior.
5. Historical studies, rejected candidates, generated outputs, model previews, and implementation records as evidence of exploration or implementation only.

Repository rules govern privacy, preservation, rights, and release practice. A model, a generated render, an old README, or a passing test cannot promote itself above the source references. A label such as “master,” “final,” or “canon” in a filename is not proof of approval.

**Conflict rule:** Do not average incompatible depictions into a new bird. Record the exact conflict, affected feature and era, reference paths, and decision owner. Apply the strongest existing scoped decision; where that does not resolve it, mark the affected criterion BLOCKED / NOT EVALUATED. Continue independent review. Never silently downgrade a fidelity requirement because the current model cannot meet it.

**Visual-twin limit:** Perspective art and short clips cannot establish every hidden surface or measurement. Match supported visible evidence at high fidelity. Identify unseen topology and mechanisms as reconstructions consistent with that evidence; obtain owner acceptance for consequential new design. Do not claim to guarantee an exact unseen backside, measured scale, or physical engineering from a raster illustration.

## 5. Scoring model and non-negotiable gates

The following numeric weights, tolerances, durations and pass thresholds are **proposed acceptance policy v1** supplied by this PRD. They operationalize owner intent; they are not quotations or previously granted owner approval. Record any owner-approved adjustment before evaluating a candidate. Never lower thresholds after seeing a failing result without a documented change decision.

### 5.1 Weighted index

| Category | Weight | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| V — Visual twin and continuity | 50 | Same MurderBird in all three eras, views and motions. |
| B — Era behavior and physical persona | 18 | Correct capability progression, grounded motion and Advanced intent. |
| C — Construction, regional skin and inspection integrity | 12 | Mechanisms and covering behave as coherent assemblies. |
| I — Spatial and visitor interaction | 8 | Legible enclosure, targeting, exploration and reversible workflows. |
| A — Access, media and resilience | 6 | Usable calm, keyboard/touch, optional media and failure paths. |
| D — Delivery, source integrity and reproducibility | 6 | Trusted, consistent, inspectable delivery of the actual solution. |
| **Total** | **100** | **No bonus points for unrequested features.** |

Score every applicable criterion for each era where listed. A row with multiple eras is evaluated separately in each; average its applicable era scores to obtain the row score. A category score is the arithmetic mean of its applicable row scores. Weighted points = category weight × category score ÷ 5. Sum weighted points for the /100 index. Categories must not be reweighted simply because evidence is absent.

The companion CSV contains one row per criterion/era with empty scores, status and evidence fields. Scores must be integers or half-points from 0 to 5. Round only displayed category/total results to two decimals; use unrounded values for gates.

### 5.2 Rating anchors

| Score | Meaning | Visual example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Absent, contradictory, unusable, or wrong subject | Crow/raptor/toy replacement, missing era, broken model. |
| 1 | Token or severely deficient implementation | Mechanical-bird theme with major identity/proportion errors. |
| 2 | Partial; substantial correction needed | Several cues match, but silhouette/head/body or era identity drifts. |
| 3 | Functional, recognizable study; material discrepancies remain | Recognizably related but visibly unlike the references in normal views or motion. |
| 4 | Strong near-match; localized visible defects remain | Identity is convincing, but specific proportion/plate/material/acting defects prevent final twin acceptance. |
| 4.5 | Acceptance-ready within documented supported evidence | No material identity mismatch in required views; only minor non-defining differences, accepted reconstruction boundaries. |
| 5 | Exemplary fidelity and continuity demonstrated | Matched evidence is consistently convincing across views, motion, close-ups, all required states and repeated review. |

Apply equivalent anchors to functional criteria: 3 means works in representative use with meaningful gaps; 4 means reliable with localized limitations; 4.5/5 requires the full stated evidence. Visual grades require actual comparison and judgment. Automated similarity scores alone cannot assign them.

### 5.3 Evidence status and coverage

Allowed status: **NOT EVALUATED**, **BLOCKED**, **SCORED**, **NOT APPLICABLE**. Record confidence separately as high/medium/low and explain the basis. A failing observed behavior is SCORED, typically 0–2; it is not “unknown.”

Unscored applicable cells contribute zero to a clearly labeled **provisional lower-bound index**, while observed-only scores and evidence coverage are also reported. They are never reported as proven failures or silently omitted to inflate results. Category coverage = scored applicable cells ÷ all applicable cells. Overall coverage = sum of category weight × category coverage ÷ 100. Gate-critical evidence must be complete even if aggregate coverage is high.

NOT APPLICABLE needs a written scope justification and evaluator approval; ordinary missing work or missing evidence is not N/A. Era-only actions are pre-scoped rather than penalizing the other eras. A change to applicability must be logged; do not remove difficult criteria after scoring begins.

| Gate | Required condition | If unmet |
| --- | --- | --- |

A 95/100 score with an inaccurate Maker head still fails G1. Passing G1 does not waive broken accessibility or source-custody controls. No document, agent majority, technical test or release action can supply the owner's missing creative acceptance.

Use outcomes: **NOT EVALUATED**, **EVALUATION BLOCKED**, **REVISION REQUIRED**, **ACCEPTANCE CANDIDATE**, **OWNER-ACCEPTED**. “Acceptance candidate” means the numeric and technical requirements are met but owner approval is outstanding. Attach release identity to any acceptance; approval does not automatically carry to changed geometry, materials or animation.

## 7. Regional anatomy and skin review contract

“Skin” means the outer material/armor assembly appropriate to each region and era. “Bones, muscle and ligaments” are explanatory analogies for frame, actuators/transmission, restraints and connections; they do not mandate flesh, biological organs, a medical autopsy, soft-body simulation or one particular rig technology.

| Region | Shape and identity to protect | Structural/motion review | Covering and inspection review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Head/crown/optic | Swept crown, head depth, circular socket, expression | Fixed skull ownership, separate jaw/neck motion, era-correct sensing | Regional plate rhythm and closed crown; processing access does not deform head. |
| Bill/mandible | Convex hooked upper bill, sharp characteristic tip and cheek opening | Plausible jaw hinge, bounded opening, reference-aligned contact surface | Distinct rigid upper/lower coverings, no jaw penetration or cheek infill. |
| Neck | Strong visible curved bridge from skull to breast | Actual base/top articulations, bounded rotation, credible links | Overlapping guards maintain gaps; no elastic collar or stacked straight pipe substitute. |
| Breast/thorax | Deep but tapered bird breast, reference mass distribution | Frame supports neck/shoulders; internal packaging fits closed envelope | Fitted irregular plate hierarchy, access cover clear of neighboring parts, true cavity. |
| Shoulder/wing/elbow | Broad compact folded mantle, tucked shielding contour | Distinct shoulder/elbow chain, limited anatomical-left travel, era-correct drive | Armor follows each rigid assembly; no human arm silhouette or long hanging fan. |
| Back/rump/rear | Compact continuation, short tail/rear outline | Body attachment and compact counterbalance; Maker external articulation | Unseen reconstruction consistent with visible sides, no dinosaur balancing tail. |
| Pelvis/hips | Birdlike load transition under breast | Hip pivots and support chain remain free; no concealed floating torso | Plates clear rotating joints and do not bridge unrelated moving bodies. |
| Thigh/shin/ankle | Substantial but articulated load-bearing legs | Appropriate passive rails, transmission or actuators per era; foot contacts | Regional guards reveal necessary articulation without generic tube legs. |
| Feet/toes/talons | Broad grounded stance, separated toes, characteristic dark curved claws | Support, swing clearance, purposeful claw action where requested | Sheaths do not engulf talon tips or change toe silhouette; no skating. |
| Power/processing/service | Distinct identity of new Advanced systems | Separate energy, distribution, sensing and processing roles | Serviceable reveals; no glowing franchise chest icon or early-era modern system leakage. |
| External support/controls | Credible Maker support without rewriting creature silhouette | Load-bearing cradle/post or equivalent; rod/line ends reach actual driven joints | External controls legible when operated and separated from creature source anatomy. |

For every region record: controlling reference, supported visible features, reconstructed features, parent assembly, pivot/axis, allowed travel, drive by era, rigid/flexible material role, attachment points, closed/open clearance, exploded behavior, known discrepancies, and acceptance owner. A material study may proceed on a region with approved geometry; a failing region must return to structure rather than receive more decorative detail.

### Chronology that must remain visible

- **Maker:** newly fabricated inherited construction before immersion; externally operated only. Dark/oiled handmade bronze and passive sockets follow the selected era evidence. No later mineral history, modern energized optic, repair upgrade, engine or cognitive system added by convenience.
- **Mechanic:** the same older body with plausible accumulated surface history and distinct industrial repair/drive parts. Separate original fabrication, age, and repair. Spring/cam implementation is a current production choice; the required outcome is constrained mechanical movement without tactical awareness.
- **Advanced:** retained inherited identity and repairs plus selective coordinated actuation, power distribution, sensing and processing. “Advanced” does not mean replacing the exterior with clean generic science-fiction armor.
- **Left shoulder:** distinguish the original fitted/restricted joint from later industrial repair hardware. Records differ in how they describe the age of particular proposed braces; retain the left-side identity and restriction, map hardware to explicit decisions, and do not silently claim an exact repair topology/date from an unclear illustration.

## 8. Visual-twin comparison procedure

### 8.1 Freeze the candidate and sources

Record commit, native/export/model hashes, material/map hashes, runtime mechanism revision, deployed URL, release manifest and reference packet. Capture the actual delivered browser model. Authoring renders, model preview stills, and marketing art may supplement it but cannot replace runtime evidence.

The reconciliation completed immediately before this PRD identified baseline `026369257553406f705ddbee41686e11cac69fa7` and model SHA-256 `3ec668b0b9bbaf1cb546ec2e04b09030c5f45b0f0893adc5b7fe5aa57baacdc5`. These are a **dated starting checkpoint**, not an assertion that a future live page or changed checkout still matches. Refresh identity at evaluation time. The earlier release verified 133 payload files plus its manifest; do not carry that count forward as a permanent expected inventory.

### 8.2 Compare equivalent views

For each era capture neutral front, rear, left, right, both three-quarter views, one elevated and one low view, plus head/bill, neck, shoulder repair, breast, wing/elbow, leg and foot details. For actual source images add a matched perspective camera approximating view angle and focal length, with the same pose when supported. Keep orthographic reconstruction sheets separately labeled; a perspective illustration is not an orthographic drawing.

Use both neutral lighting and intended exhibit lighting. For silhouette comparisons remove backgrounds/labels from the comparison presentation without altering the source subject. Align on supported landmarks and use a uniform scale/translation/rotation; never nonuniformly warp a candidate to hide proportion defects. Preserve complete uncropped originals beside any diagnostic crop or overlay.

### 8.3 Measure without pretending art is metrology

Proposed diagnostic triggers: silhouette intersection-over-union below 0.90, median visible-landmark displacement above 2% of image-space body height, or key proportion deviation above 5% warrants explicit review. Record mask quality, occlusion, landmark definitions and camera uncertainty. Use these only when images are sufficiently comparable. A result outside a tolerance is a review flag; a result inside it is not artistic acceptance. Do not invent a measurement for an occluded feature or enforce a 2D ratio across mismatched perspective.

Assess defining features individually: bill depth/curvature, crown sweep, eye scale/recess, cheek opening, head/neck/breast balance, folded mantle span, breast taper, leg/foot proportions, rear contour, plate rhythm, left-side distinction and era surface history. A serious mismatch in any one of these can block G1 even if the silhouette metric is favorable.

### 8.4 Review movement as continuity, not isolated good frames

Play source clips at normal speed first, then use timecoded frames and slow playback for diagnosis. Separate source-camera motion from creature motion. Compare stable proportions, plate continuity, joint relationships, weight, characteristic posture and material response through the entire usable source sequence. A single matched opening frame does not establish video continuity.

Record uncut runtime sequences for each era; include all mandatory actions, the transitions between them, and a complete inspection/reassembly cycle. Look for shape changes, rubber metal, detached panels, foot skating, discontinuous lighting/materials and identity loss at extremes. Novel cage/jump/thrust choreography must preserve source identity and be labeled new exhibit action rather than historical footage reproduction.

### 8.5 Independent observation and owner decision

Recommended review order: evaluator records defects before seeing prior numeric scores; a second reviewer checks the same frozen evidence; unresolved material disagreement is recorded rather than averaged away; the owner makes the per-era likeness decision. Keep observed defects, proposed fixes, numeric grades and owner approval as separate fields. This workflow does not require multiple agents or a particular model provider.

### Proposed performance targets

Use a named representative supported desktop and physical mobile device. Aim for sustained desktop median frame time ≤16.7 ms and p95 ≤33.3 ms; mobile median ≤33.3 ms and p95 ≤50 ms over a two-minute encounter after load. Essential input feedback should appear within 100 ms under the test conditions. Present useful loading/status content within two seconds and target an interactive scene within ten seconds on a documented 25 Mbps / 50 ms latency test profile. Report cold and warm runs separately.

These are review targets, not claims about the current product. Measure stutters, peak observed memory and model transfer cost; do not equate an eight-second rolling sample with sustained performance or estimated texture memory with measured device VRAM. If these targets need adjustment, agree the actual device/network contract before scoring. Do not remove defining geometry to pass performance while failing the visual-twin gate. No dependency or engine replacement is implied.

## 10. Defect severity and correction order

| Severity | Meaning | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P0 — Gate blocker | Wrong character/era identity; major defining-feature mismatch; prohibited early-era capability; broken essential journey; private-data exposure; wrong delivered revision | Stop acceptance of affected candidate. Address before presentation polish. |
| P1 — Major | Clear shape/material/acting mismatch or recurring mechanical/interaction defect in ordinary use | Correct before visual-twin/final acceptance. |
| P2 — Moderate | Localized issue that does not redefine identity but is visible or harms usability | Track correction and retest; may prevent 4.5/5. |
| P3 — Minor | Small non-defining polish issue with no material continuity/access impact | Record and judge against owner expectations; not an automatic waiver. |

Correction sequence: resolve reference/authority conflicts → fix silhouette and proportions → correct head/neck/mantle/limb structure → validate pivots and motion ranges → fit regional rigid covering → match materials and wear → refine acting and contact → prove inspection restoration → validate access/performance → verify release identity → seek owner acceptance. Work may overlap across independently stable regions, but unresolved structural defects cannot be buried under textures.

A finding must name the era, region/state, criterion, reference view/timecode, observed runtime evidence, visible discrepancy, severity, correction intent and retest. “Looks off,” “more realistic,” and “make it better” are insufficient findings.
