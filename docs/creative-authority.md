## Era and heart/brain separation

The owner’s requirement that earlier generations have no heart or brain can be reconciled with the story by defining those as the later, named onboard energy and learning systems. It must not erase the early body’s unexplained mechanisms or rewrite ambiguous behavior as a proven artificial intelligence. In visitor-facing text, “brain” should mean the 2025 cranial processing/learning assembly, not a biological organ or a confirmed description of the earlier eye-chamber assembly.

## Cage and aggression discrepancy

The mandate’s cage is a new exhibit boundary, not established story scenery. None of the selected full-body references, the selected era previews, or the owner-accepted First Choice study shows a cage. Older attack/warning compositions and the September camera-series action prompts are historical pose studies; they do not provide a coherent, accepted cage-contact animation or prove that the story’s quiet Sentinel ending should become an attack.

Resolve this by treating the cage as a separable museum-exhibit fixture around the established creature. The visitor may deliberately trigger a bounded, recoverable threat display or cage contact in the interactive exhibit, consistent with the mandate. Keep the story’s Maker ambiguity, inert Water interval, finite Mechanic movement, and modern learning distinction intact. Do not present a new snap/claw event as a recovered story fact. Cage material, spacing, collision path, safe reach zone, visitor input affordance, and contact sound remain proposals requiring a visual and interaction review.

## Production decisions and open owner-facing choices

Evidence supports using candidate 03 as the current visible identity target, with the July owner selection limited to head form and 2.0 m as a lead-selected production target. It also supports keeping heart and mind separate and preserving uncertainty about the ancient mechanism. These decisions do not need to be reopened to begin a bounded blockout.

Consequential choices that remain open for review are: (1) the cage’s visual language and whether visitors encounter the bird through bars or a more abstract containment boundary; (2) the intensity and exact contact point of the visitor-triggered reaction; (3) a dedicated, close-up reference for the inherited left-shoulder repair; and (4) the proposed, noncanonical geometry of unseen winding, power, and processing assemblies. These are design decisions, not missing historical facts.
