## Claw and autonomous choreography

Advanced has a named **Claw scrape** control and one corresponding autonomous plan. Its six stages are approach, lift, contact, scrape, release and recovery. It shifts toward the opposite support foot, raises and extends the acting foot, articulates the digits, positions their actual distal meshes at the floor, performs a short floor scrape, releases and replants. This establishes a kinematic supported floor-contact action, not a demonstrated rail grip, forces, traction or real physical balance.

An independent verifier caught transient floor penetration and abrupt ankle travel in early implementations. The final action measures exported distal mesh height throughout the action, uses a staged descent/curl, keeps the opposite support target fixed and re-captures recovery from the actual interrupted pose. Pause holds the pose. Inspection and reduced motion recover before continuing their requested state; era changes and reset clear the old action.

The three existing cage-test families now have visibly different timing envelopes: one short edge probe, a gradual sustained rail press and separated seam probes. The shared geometric rail-contact solver retains its physical thresholds. Unattended testing exposed a full-turn heading bug: the contact gate rejected ±2π even when aligned. The corrected gate normalizes the angle; an actual-model regression verifies the same rail contact at 0 and ±2π. Earlier runtime captures and the failed report remain preserved under `runtime-iterations/pre-yaw-wrap-9b8a03d00e30/`. Recent-plan avoidance and weighted family selection replace the forced three-family cycle. Selected intentions/routes are recorded separately from actions that actually began or reached contact.

A DEV-only `review-seed` parameter supplies reproducible fresh recording seeds. Production exposes neither that test hook nor the review API. Manual claw controls remain disabled in early eras, fallback, pause and conflicting transitions. Active claw recovery text remains visible when reduced motion is requested.
