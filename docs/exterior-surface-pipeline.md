# Exterior surface pipeline — v1 proposal

Status: editable local production study. The current source has been rendered and checked locally; earlier rejected working geometry is preserved separately. No likeness, material, or owner acceptance is implied. The existing Vite, Three.js, and Web Audio architecture remains in place, with no new application dependency.

## Source and authoring boundary

[`assets/models/uncaged-exterior-v1/`](../assets/models/uncaged-exterior-v1/) contains the editable Blender source, combined runtime GLB, individual era exports, and regional texture maps. `scripts/exterior-head-neck.py` and `scripts/exterior-body-regions.py` author the fitted surfaces; `scripts/build-uncaged-exterior-v1.py` assigns UVs, materials, era copies, and GLB export. Original image and story sources remain preserved in their existing paths. The [regional map](exterior-regional-map.md) records the current component proposal; the [stage record](exterior-stage-record.md) distinguishes implementation evidence from artistic acceptance.

The selected candidate 03 full-body illustration controls the shared stance, breast, folded flightless wing mass, legs, and rear. The July head image controls the crown, cheek opening, and hooked bill. Maker-clean informs the newly fabricated early shell; Mechanic imagery informs later repair and inherited wear; the Heart image supports only an opened chest view. These images are not engineering drawings. Hidden topology, exact dimensions, alloy, chemistry, wear chronology, repairs, fastener spacing, and construction remain proposed.

Blender stores editable rigid parts and three era configurations. The combined GLB keeps separate copies by attachment, era eligibility, and region; three individual era GLBs are review exports. Runtime-only movement systems remain authored in Three.js: Maker external controls, the Mechanic spring/reduction/cam transmission, and Builder/Advanced distribution and actuators. They are not presented as measured physical machinery. Existing rig joints and pivots are retained. The GLB carries standard glTF metallic-roughness textures; no authoring-only shader is required in the browser.

## UVs and materials

Each 1024×1024 atlas has a 4×4 grid of 256-pixel tiles. Eight tiles cover head, neck, breast, shoulder, wing, pelvis, leg, and back shell fields; the remaining role tiles cover bill, frame, bearing, edge, repair, dark, optic, and ceramic. Each object’s vertex bounds are computed once. Polygon faces select a projection axis from their normals and project through that object-level bound into the assigned tile with a gutter. This is not per-polygon bounds remapping, but projection axes can change across faces and object-level normalization does not guarantee a uniform physical texel density across differently sized parts. It is a regional/role atlas, not a photographic full-body bake.

The seven embedded maps are Maker, Mechanic, and Builder base-color/ORM pairs plus a shared tangent normal map. Blender exports standard glTF base color, roughness, metallic, and tangent-space normal inputs. The current generator uses .42 normal strength and regional shell roughness variation. Talons use the edge/high-wear role; bearing, bill, repair, optic, dark, and ceramic surfaces retain separate material roles. The aged shell currently uses the same inherited base treatment for Mechanic and Builder, with era distinction supplied by separately gated repair, optics, service details, and equipment. This follows the inheritance proposal and does not assert exact finish chemistry.

Patina, forging marks, and edge wear remain procedural visual proposals. The current aged deposit is driven by atlas UV position and tile-edge proximity, not by a modeled seam/cavity mask or gravity-aware surface analysis. It should not be described as proven physical corrosion placement. The combined three-era GLB is 16.17 MiB. Its transfer limit is 18 MiB, revised from the initial 16 MiB target to retain the conforming plate geometry and actual layer thickness. The seven maps still estimate 37.33 MiB decoded RGBA plus mipmaps, below the unchanged 48 MiB texture limit. Runtime estimates are not device VRAM measurements.

## Era eligibility and runtime assembly

Each exported surface copy carries `exteriorEras`, `region`, and `surfaceRole` glTF extras. Export batching joins parts by attachment, era and region while preserving material primitives. A joined node’s `surfaceRole` is therefore representative only; use the source inventory and material primitives for detailed assignments. The generic authoring `inherited` boolean is also non-authoritative in this frozen version: it incorrectly remains true on some Advanced-only lens/service additions. Use the explicit regional inheritance table, piece names and era eligibility to interpret chronology; runtime gating does not use that boolean. Runtime visibility follows the eligibility tag, while power, processing, optics, and later repair assemblies keep their independent era gates. A surface tag never grants visibility to a functional subsystem. This prevents early Maker/Mechanic states from inheriting Builder equipment just because geometry shares a region.

The protected Builder distribution case is attached to the body at local `[-0.21, 0.29, 0.07]`; it is smaller and separately placed from the power core. Its three-segment branch route passes behind the core before connecting to visible targets. The module reports era visibility and selected connection/separation metrics. This is a kinematic exhibit assembly: link movement, endpoints, and disconnected sockets do not establish delivered force, efficiency, collision freedom, or a physical power model.

The browser reads PBR maps through the existing Three.js loader and records eligible/visible surface objects by region, texture dimensions and memory estimate. A neutral/exhibit lighting switch and fixed review cameras exist only through the Vite development hook; production output omits that hook. The illustrated fallback uses local fixed model preview images and keeps the separate assembly diagrams. It is a still-image and text view, not an animated 3D substitute.

## Verification and limits

The source placement validator samples outer wing-plate vertices against their curved backing and checks that legacy solid thigh tubes are absent. It is a rest-pose sample, not a continuous collision solver or likeness test. The current receipt passes 56 sampled overlays and the legacy-tube retirement check. The failed iteration-B receipt remains in `provisional/` with its rejected renders. Zero-area bevel-tip faces are removed before export to keep contact queries defined; finite vertex attributes are checked in each GLB.

Use the dated final receipts for checks actually completed. Reproduction uses the source/export validators, `npm ci`, `npm run build`, and headed browser checks against the exact served GLB. Inspect the full `dist/` allowlist and asset resolution; review neutral and exhibit views from matched cameras, all three era states, front/side/rear silhouettes, head/neck/anatomical-left shoulder/breast/feet closeups, inspection/separation, representative motion poses, and the illustrated fallback. Record browser/hardware, viewport, pixel ratio, timing window, screenshots, and the fetched GLB hash. The pre-existing Google Tag Manager request is stubbed locally with an empty response during QA; this does not validate analytics and no model content is uploaded to an external service.
