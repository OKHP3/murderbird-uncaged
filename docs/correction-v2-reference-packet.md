## V10 — anatomical-left restriction and repair chronology

**Confirmed story evidence:** the Maker’s left-shoulder plate was fitted backward, removed, and fitted again; its edge remains different. In the Mechanic chapter, an iron brace carries a load the old bronze cannot, and the 1873-era left-wing description places wedge-shaped marks under a Victorian repair strap. In the Advanced chapter, the Builder opens the shoulder, finds the twice-fitted plate, and the system learns to respect the short travel instead of forcing symmetry. The story establishes an original Maker asymmetry, later industrial work, and Advanced inheritance. It does not provide orthographic views, dimensions, exact attachment topology, or a measured travel stop.

The text is from the preserved local snapshot [`content/story/index.main.html`](../content/story/index.main.html), SHA-256 `89564989c55cfb2a6315763a76172ea9643e9dc29fc388e636f7849330593c7d` (44,864 bytes), imported from OverKill Hill revision `f1142a02f1e73cd45e46e981022aef27f1c6e095` as recorded in [`provenance/overkill-hill-import-2026-09-26.json`](../provenance/overkill-hill-import-2026-09-26.json). This identifies the snapshot used for textual evidence; it is not a claim about the current live page.

**Two reviewable interpretations:**

1. **Original plate is the restriction; later brace is repair/load path (recommended draft).** Maker has the twice-fitted plate and a distinct, small left travel envelope. Mechanic adds a visible iron strap/brace and brass bearing across or adjacent to that plate to carry the later load; it must clear rather than erase the original plate’s limit. Advanced retains both, with the actuator/controller stopping at the inherited limit. This follows the strongest chronology in the story text.
2. **Original plate sets asymmetry; later brace shares the restriction.** Maker still has the twice-fitted plate and unequal left motion. Mechanic adds the visible brace and bearing; the new hardware may also become a secondary stop, provided the original plate remains identifiable as the cause of the asymmetric fit. Advanced respects the combined limit. This is plausible but adds a mechanical role the story does not state.

The diagram [`left-shoulder-chronology-options.svg`](../assets/audit/neutral-v2/decisions/left-shoulder-chronology-options.svg) shows the layer sequence and marks its attachment geometry as proposed. Both options preserve anatomical left, a short travel range, the original plate across eras, and visible later Mechanic repair. Neither option mirrors the repair to the right side or claims real force capacity. The production recommendation is option 1 until the owner chooses otherwise; the owner decision remains pending.

**Source comparison:** Maker-clean supports original era material and fitted plate treatment; the Mechanic still and clip support later repair context; the Water image records intervening patina; the Advanced stills and story preserve the same recognizable bird and later understanding of the restriction. None of the illustrations alone proves a hard-stop design. The older mass-study/camera references that depict asymmetry are studies, not additional controlling authorities.
