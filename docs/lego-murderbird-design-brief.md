# Manual LEGO MurderBird Design Brief

Owner direction recorded October 1, 2026: replace the proposed powered animatronic build with an affordable, manually movable mixture of LEGO Technic and conventional bricks. This brief governs the future physical LEGO interpretation, after the ongoing character work reaches a useful checkpoint. It does not change the interactive exhibit or its era motion contracts.

## Accepted scope

Use a Technic frame for strength, joints, and any useful manual transmission. Use conventional bricks, plates, slopes, and selected Technic panels for the face and armor. Movement comes from positioning the model by hand or operating a small crank or knob. Omit motors, hubs, batteries, sensors, powered lights, and electronic controls.

Here, advanced means an articulated construction with deliberate mechanisms. It does not select the Advanced era as the initial appearance. The first build should depict one selected era; interchangeable era details can follow if the core model fits the budget.

## Proposed design targets

The owner selected approximately 14 inches tall. With an assumed 7-foot (84-inch) full-size MurderBird, this is exactly 1:6 scale: 35.56 cm from feet to crown, excluding a display support. The full-size height is an owner-supplied premise, not a recovered dimension from the source models. The earlier 35 cm shorthand is approximately 1:6.1.

The remaining targets are design proposals, not proven quantities, prices, construction, or owner acceptance. See the [recent model review](lego-murderbird-35cm-model-review.md) for measured source proportions and their limits.

- Design around the 35.56 cm target. A literal scaling of the latest V38 whole-body study gives an approximately 19.3 cm wide by 23.2 cm deep saved-pose envelope. Brick construction and useful articulation may require proportion adjustments.
- Explore roughly 1,200–1,800 pieces for the first bird and a simple removable support. Let the priced inventory and acceptable likeness determine whether that target holds.
- Make head turn, neck pitch, jaw opening, wing guard positioning, hips, and knees the first articulation targets. Add ankle, toe, and tail articulation only where the joints remain compact and useful.
- Use joints that hold a pose under the finished armor's weight. Retain a removable support as an option; standing and balancing through the proposed pose range remain unverified.
- Consider one manual linkage for jaw or neck movement after proving the direct joints. Do not assume that every joint needs a gearbox.
- Preserve the flightless silhouette, constructed bill, crown, compact mantle, substantial legs, and talons. Reflect the repaired anatomical left shoulder's limited travel in the chosen reference.

## Budget and purchasing

The owner's dollar ceiling and reusable LEGO inventory are not established. A budget question is pending. Do not present a particular spending level as authorized or claim that this design can meet it before producing a priced parts list.

Prioritize common available elements and colors. Use inexpensive colors inside the frame, reserve the visible palette for the armor, and avoid making rare metallic finishes essential to likeness. Separate the final model inventory from experimental and spare purchases. Track shipping, tax, and any display support in the total. No purchases are authorized by this brief.

## Design sequence

1. Select the controlling reference and initial era after the parallel character work reaches its intended checkpoint. Preserve source assets and their acceptance labels.
2. Test the characteristic head, bill, and neck joint at a small scale. Show a matched-view comparison before committing to whole-body proportions.
3. Design the frame, hips, knees, feet, and optional support. Check that the proposed pose range carries the expected head and armor mass.
4. Add removable breast and shoulder armor. Check joint clearance throughout the actual proposed motion range.
5. Prepare an editable brick model, assembly sequence, and inventory using the previously researched Studio or LeoCAD workflow. Verify exact part and color availability and price the complete shopping list.
6. Physically prototype the loaded joints before treating the digital model or instructions as buildable. Reduce scope or revise construction if it misses the budget or cannot hold its poses.

## Completion evidence

The eventual design package should contain an editable brick model, assembly instructions, a priced inventory, front and side likeness comparisons, and physical evidence that the major joints hold their poses. Record remaining support, clearance, sourcing, and appearance limitations separately. A render establishes appearance only; a digital connection check does not establish physical strength.

The present artifact is a scope brief. CAD assembly, parts sourcing, physical testing, buildability, cost, and owner likeness acceptance remain unverified.
