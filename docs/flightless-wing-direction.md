# Flightless wing and shove direction

This note records the owner's clarification for the local shield-study derivative. It controls earlier descriptions of the wings as merely ornamental. It does not change story sources, establish engineering facts, approve final art, or authorize publication.

## Owner's exact wording

> Reminder that the bird is flightless and the wings are more for balance and shielding
>
> Think of a football running back tucking the ball in but using the shoulder and elbow to push off and shove

## Confirmed creative intent

- MurderBird is flightless. The wings serve balance and shielding; they are not flight surfaces.
- The action should read as a compact, forceful shove: the body tucks in behind a guarded shoulder, then uses the shoulder and elbow to push off and shove.
- Keep the wings compact and integrated with the bird's heavy terrestrial body. Their guard posture should shield the ribcage rather than open into an airborne silhouette.
- Preserve the established asymmetry and the inherited anatomical left-shoulder repair with limited travel. Do not mirror away that history or assume both shoulders have equal range.

## Proposed articulation for the shield study

Treat each wing as a short articulated guard assembly with a shoulder pivot, upper segment, visible elbow joint, and forearm/outer guard plates. Fold the upper segment against the ribcage as if tucking an object close; use the elbow and armored forearm as the leading shove surface. The far-side wing and planted lower body can counterbalance the compact push. Keep the movement asymmetric and bounded, then let the assembly return to its tucked guard pose.

This joint layout and pose are proposals for the derivative, not recovered historical construction or a confirmed character rig. A deliberately readable shoulder-to-elbow chain is required for the study to test the owner's direction; naming a single broad `mantle` group is not enough to prove that articulation exists. The exact pushing side and movement range remain open to visual review, with the left shoulder's documented restriction retained.

## Motion and force limits

Keep feet grounded and the body's weight visibly supported during the shove. The motion can use a small counter-rotation or weight shift, a short shoulder/elbow drive toward the enclosure, and a restrained return. Do not animate lift, takeoff, hovering, gliding, wing-assisted running, or flapping. Do not turn the clarification into an escape event or an attack unrelated to the enclosure interaction.

The reference and owner wording establish creative intent only. They do not verify center of mass, joint strength, actuator design, contact force, injury risk, cage deflection, or physically feasible balance. Any shove remains a staged exhibit pose until reviewed; the animation and cage response are not physics or safety certification.

## Review checks

1. In a neutral pose, read the wings as folded balance/shield guards on a flightless heavy bird.
2. In the shove pose, clearly read the tucked shoulder, articulated elbow, ribcage shield, planted support, and short outward push.
3. Compare both sides without erasing the left shoulder's repaired, limited-travel character. Record any asymmetry that cannot be reconciled from current references as unresolved.
4. Confirm there is no airborne or flapping cue, and that the pose returns to the guarded rest state.
5. Keep the derivative labeled as a proposal pending owner likeness and motion acceptance.
