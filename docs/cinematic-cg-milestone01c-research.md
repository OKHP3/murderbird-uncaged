| Tier | Evidence and implication |
|---|---|
| Confirmed in paper | Predominantly terrestrial birds have a more rearward and lower centre of mass than predominantly flying birds in the sampled comparison (Results, PDF p.3). |
| Confirmed in paper | Body shape and mass distribution do not follow a simple dinosaur-versus-bird split (Abstract; Discussion). Shape alone does not establish mechanical balance. |
| Confirmed in paper | The study standardises posture; necks are extended. A sensitivity test with bent necks changes mass position (Methods, PDF p.8). Its figures are not prescriptions for a live standing pose. |
| Owner direction | Embedded lower-third hips, compact posterior torso, short thick neck and enlarged folded shields come from the owner-reviewed geometry, not a universal rule in the paper. |
| Design proposal | A fictional dense power/transmission assembly below and behind the hips could offset the forward head/beak load. No masses, torque or dynamic stability have been validated. |

## Application in 1c

Use study05's joint anchors and torso profiles; dress that geometry with
mechanical layers. Keep the knees forward of the hips, hocks behind the knees
and feet planted. Preserve the low posterior taper rather than a spherical
human-like pelvis. The folded wings remain ornamental shields. Any visible
deviation from the approved scaffold must be named in the checkpoint receipt.
The reference artwork controls identity and finish; the paper informs the
stance rationale and its limits.
