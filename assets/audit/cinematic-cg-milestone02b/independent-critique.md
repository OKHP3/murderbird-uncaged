## Judgment

**INFERRED visual judgment:** the candidate has made a visible move away from the rejected anteater reading and toward a recognizable mechanical bird. It remains substantially short of the specific locked MurderBird likeness and its nightmare presence. The main remaining problem is construction and regional shape, rather than insufficient darkness or detail count.

## Visible gains

The deep hooked upper bill and explicit lower-jaw separation give the head a stronger avian landmark. The breast/wing opening exposes machinery instead of reading as one continuous barrel. Added rods and joint discs make the legs more articulated than the previous thin chain-like forms. These changes are visible in the neutral comparison; they do not depend solely on dramatic lighting.

## What brute force has and has not achieved

The expanded effort has produced independently useful visible corrections: stronger bird landmarks, an opened flank and more explicit leg hardware. It has not yet reproduced the source's specific head, compact shield-wing, layered neck/body integration, dense regional construction or heavy predatory feet. Worker count and detail accumulation are not evidence of convergence. The candidate is still a CG proposal; owner artistic acceptance, physical balance and final three-era fidelity are unknown.

## Next artistic direction

**PROPOSAL:** concentrate the next bounded increment on a matched neutral head-and-shoulder study. Establish crown/brow/cheek/bill-root and compact wing boundaries together, then build a small number of source-matched mechanical clusters at the cheek, throat and flank. Keep the visible leg gains. Show that study beside the locked image before another broad surface pass. A darker grade or more scratches may improve mood, but cannot resolve these silhouette and construction gaps.
