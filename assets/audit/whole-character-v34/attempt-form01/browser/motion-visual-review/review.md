# Browser motion visual review

Source: `../full-era-mechanisms.webm` (43.042945 s per ffprobe; 856×648 VP9 browser recording). The preserved `manifest.json` binds the video and metadata hashes to 18 ffmpeg-extracted stills and their presentation times. This is a visual spot check of those sampled frames only.

- **Maker controls:** The leg frames show the foot/leg mechanism changing against the support rail; the wing pair changes from a more raised to a lowered pose; and the neck views show a modest head/neck turn. Those motions remain visibly connected in these samples. The wing-root and rear neck transitions expose dark gaps, and the leg mechanism remains visually dominated by conspicuous transverse axle hardware. The individual control demonstrations are restrained rather than large excursions.
- **Mechanic step:** Early, middle, and late frames show a clear change in stepping stance and body position. The feet remain attached and the movement reads as a step sequence in the selected samples. Large roller-like knee/leg assemblies dominate the silhouette, but no gross separation is visible.
- **Advanced jump:** The selected early/mid/late frames do not clearly show an airborne jump: the feet remain close to the floor and the body-height change is not legible enough to verify a leap from these stills. The recording segment is labeled “Advanced jump,” but that label alone does not establish the movement visually.
- **Advanced thrust:** The bird visibly shifts toward the target ring and changes stance across the three sampled frames. The samples support a visible approach/brace-like action, though they do not establish its full travel or contact with the target.
- **Advanced strike/recovery:** The late samples show a large vertical/body-position change and changing leg posture; the sequence is visibly active. The exact strike event is not unambiguously legible from these three stills alone.

Across the reviewed frames I saw no obvious grossly detached body part. The repeated visual defects are the bulky transverse leg axles, dark gaps at wing/torso and neck transitions, and the still-simplified shell-like body/head. This review does not establish continuous motion quality, surface clearance, collision absence, physical behavior, or artistic acceptance. The recording metadata also identifies the capture as a sample demonstration rather than physical simulation.
