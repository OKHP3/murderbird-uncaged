**Disposition: revise the shared envelope before segmenting it.** This is a useful contour direction, not a finished neck/breast form.

In the side view, the new neck-to-chest outline makes a real change: below the jaw, the throat narrows and then the breast turns forward below it. That is closer to the owner target's curved transition than V20's vertical throat ending at a collar shelf. The rear neck line also sweeps toward the shoulder more clearly. Keep that shared-contour idea.

The forward turn currently becomes a large, nearly spherical breast bulb with a pointed lower end. It hangs below the throat as a separate mass instead of merging into the torso's forward-full chest. In front view the envelope resolves into a central shield and two side lobes, with conspicuous triangular dark gaps at the throat/shoulder and between the breast and its side structure. The three-quarter and side views show the same openings as exposed voids, not narrow service seams. The nape/upper-back transition still has a visible break behind the neck; the shoulder does not read as one continuous support into the mantle.

The owner target's breast is substantial, but its contour feeds into the body without an isolated pendant silhouette. Before adding plate subdivisions, reduce and flatten the breast's most protruding central volume, blend its lower/rear contour into the torso, and close the unintended triangular throat/shoulder voids with the shared envelope. Carry the nape into the shoulder/upper back as a continuous sweep. Preserve only intentional, bounded access openings. Once the silhouette and continuity read correctly in side, front, and three-quarter views, move on to segmentation and plate detail.

This is a neutral-render visual judgment against a perspective owner reference. It does not establish clearance, runtime motion, or likeness acceptance; no technical check was used as an art criterion.
