# Alignment v3 — independent visual review

**Assessment: revision required in all three eras.** The candidate contains visible regional construction improvements, but the current neutral geometry does not reproduce the defining head, plate hierarchy and limb construction of the selected illustrations. This is an independent static-image critique, not owner acceptance, a new full score, or a motion/collision certificate.

Native authoring views do not contain all browser-generated era mechanisms and cannot certify their integration.

## Observed changes and discrepancies

| Region | Visible progress | Remaining discrepancy and next evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Head, cheek and bill root** | The optic now has a continuous shaped surround with a descending cheek arm reaching the jaw articulation. The previous tiny disconnected cheek bridge is no longer the principal connection. The hooked upper bill remains legible, with a longitudinal ridge and a substantial root. | In the [head close-up](../assets/audit/alignment-v3/alignment-head.png), the surround and descending arm read as a broad flat cutout, while the small bill-root side cap interrupts their flow into the cere. The opening below the optic is a large dark rectangular passage in the three-quarter view. July instead shows nested curved cheek members around a more deliberately shaped negative space. The bill's smooth, regular outer sweep still simplifies the reference's deep convex face and tighter inner cutting profile. Refine the cheek cross-section and the opening as a connected composition; do not simply fill the opening or enlarge the optic ring. |
| **Mandible** | The lower jaw has a solid curved side face and an upturned tip; it no longer reads as the v2 round wire. The rear articulation is identifiable. | The present jaw is still a broad leaf-like slab with relatively straight central edges. July's lower jaw reads as a narrower curved arm with a distinct inner contour and a more gradual change of mass toward the tip. Its correct closed relationship to the upper bill and its unobstructed sweep cannot be judged from the rest renders. Require closed/open side views and the full motion before crediting the articulation repair. |
| **Crown and temporal covering** | The central cap now spans the roof; it is no longer only an open pair of narrow arches. The optic is less dominated by the detached washer appearance of v2. | The [bilateral profiles](../assets/audit/alignment-v3/alignment-side-right.png) and head close-up show a few broad straps descending along the side/rear, with wide open gaps and exposed dark backing between them. This gives the head a helmet-like construction rather than the source's many compact backswept laminae and a few broader cap pieces. Replacing the old repetitive shingles has not yet established the source's crown identity. Rear construction is inferred, but the visible side profile must still support the selected head silhouette. |
| **Body, breast and neck** | The deep tapered breast and compact body proportions remain. Breast pieces are larger and less uniformly tiled than v2; the neck has curved frontal covering and some lateral guard mass. | The [breast close-up](../assets/audit/alignment-v3/alignment-breast.png) shows sparse polygonal patches divided by broad smooth shell gutters. Their courses remain readily counted horizontal bands, rather than a regional hierarchy of overlapping central shields and side transitions. Both profiles show a conspicuous long rear/side neck strut and an abrupt head-to-neck gap. The selected illustrations have layered curved construction carrying mass through that transition. Preserve the provisional envelope while refining plate cross-sections, roots and overlap direction; surface wear cannot supply the missing construction. |
| **Shoulder and mantle** | The compact folded envelope remains; coverage reaches down the flank and the lowest plate tips produce a stepped edge. A separate broad upper region and longer lower pieces are visible. | The [mantle close-up](../assets/audit/alignment-v3/alignment-mantle.png) still reads primarily as a rounded pod with several fairly regular courses. The reference has a stronger progression from small rounded shoulder coverts to longer distal plates. Fine crossing slivers/edges occur around several plate roots in the render; their cause needs geometry inspection before calling them interpenetration. The smooth inner shoulder/back surfaces and exposed connection region remain conspicuous in the rear view, whose unseen construction is proposed. No static view establishes shoulder clearance or the restricted left travel. |
| **Legs, ankle guards, feet and talons** | The [feet close-up](../assets/audit/alignment-v3/alignment-feet.png) shows guards with rounded cross-sections, tapered ends and a continuous curved ankle-to-foot cover. This is clearer shaped coverage than v2's flat rectangular front panels. Individual digit links and hooked tips remain visible. | Large plain drum joints and thin exposed side rods still dominate the legs. The long smooth ankle cover has little of the selected illustrations' articulated sheath construction. The talons have bulbous curled roots and slender downward tips, while the source feet read as substantial tapered digits with distinct hard claw sheaths. Digit spread and tip height do not establish load support, grip or purposeful claw contact. Preserve hinge independence while refining mass transitions; confirm plantar support and swept clearance in the browser. |

## Era-specific reading

The [Maker perspective](../assets/audit/alignment-v3/maker-reference-perspective.png) retains a dark optic and the shared grounded bird outline. The [Mechanic perspective](../assets/audit/alignment-v3/mechanic-reference-perspective.png) presents the same neutral structural identity. The [Advanced perspective](../assets/audit/alignment-v3/builder-reference-perspective.png) includes the warm optic center. All three inherit the head, breast, mantle and limb discrepancies above. These deliberately neutral views do not test Maker finish, Mechanic repair chronology, Advanced wear, or the runtime control/transmission/power systems. Their similarities are not evidence that those systems are missing from the browser.

The revised cheek and limb covers are concrete construction gains. They do not establish a net completed likeness, and the broad crown strips introduce their own mismatch. F01–F04 remain open; the dependent surface pass remains inappropriate as a way to hide unresolved geometry.
