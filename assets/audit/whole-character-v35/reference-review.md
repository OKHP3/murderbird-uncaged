# Whole-form reference review for V35

**Controlling views.** The owner’s whole-bird image controls torso, mantle, hip roots, and legs. The July image is head-only guidance; it should not be used to infer body proportions. These are differently composed perspective images, so the notes below describe visible relationships rather than physical measurements. V34 renders are neutral gray, which limits material and finish comparisons; this review concerns shape and construction.

1. **Chest-to-pelvis continuity:** In the owner image, the armored breast remains full through the upper body, then draws into a visually connected lower trunk and the two leg roots. V34 reads as a large tiled egg ending above a conspicuous horizontal axle/crossbar. Rework the lower breast termination and hip-root transition as one continuous load-bearing silhouette, so the thighs emerge from a formed pelvis rather than appearing hung from a bar. Keep the breast’s substantial front volume; reducing it alone would miss the issue.

2. **Mantle-to-body attachment:** The owner’s folded mantle is a compact, overlapping shoulder mass that wraps from the nape onto the upper sides of the breast. V34’s shoulder caps sit outboard with visible triangular dark openings at their inner roots, especially in front view, making the wings look detached from the trunk. Bring the inner mantle returns into the shoulder/nape structure and close the unresolved void while retaining the folded, short-wing outline. The reference does show mechanical gaps, but these read as joints within an attached shoulder assembly, not openings separating the shoulder from the body.

3. **Leg joint scale and direction:** The reference legs read as substantial vertical mechanisms: large round bearings are embedded in successive shaped members, with the load path continuing into broad feet. V34 exposes oversized horizontal axle cylinders across the hip/knee line, while thinner diagonal bars carry the shins; in front view those transverse cylinders become the dominant leg silhouette. Reduce their visual projection and integrate each bearing into a deeper, aligned upper/lower member. Preserve visible articulation, but make the vertical load path—not the cross-axle—the primary shape.

These are proportions and attachment corrections, not requests for extra surface detail. They are visual priorities for the next candidate, not an acceptance judgment.

**Priority note for the next body pass:** Do not narrow the upper breast as the first response to the broad silhouette. The owner reference also carries substantial chest volume; the clearer mismatch is the exposed flank/root break and the unconnected lower breast-to-hip transition. Close and integrate those edges before testing a modest mass change. The owner image may also depict a larger head-and-bill envelope relative to the trunk than V34, but that is a separate head decision: cranial scale can be adjusted around its neck attachment independently of bill-profile design, provided jaw and neck contacts are checked afterward.
